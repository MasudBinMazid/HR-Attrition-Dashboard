🌟 HR Attrition Dashboard

🎯 Why We Built This Dashboard

Our people are at the heart of our organization. This project takes a closer look at who is leaving, why they might be leaving, and where we can do better. By turning numbers into stories, we can shape stronger retention strategies, improve employee engagement, and create a better workplace for everyone.

👥 The Big Picture

We have 1,470 employees in total.

237 employees left the company, giving us an attrition rate of 16.1%.

That means 1,233 employees are still with us, and our average employee age is 37 years.
📌 This tells us we have a healthy mix of experienced employees, but we’re losing nearly 1 in 6 team members—this deserves attention.



🏢 Where We’re Losing Talent

Sales has the highest turnover. This could be due to job stress, competition, or growth opportunities elsewhere.

Technical roles like Laboratory Technicians and Sales Executives are the most affected.

📌 If we focus on supporting these roles, we can make the biggest difference.



🎓 Education & Growth

Most employees leaving hold Bachelor’s and Master’s degrees.
📌 These are ambitious professionals likely seeking growth—this is a chance to invest in career development and leadership pathways.



📊 Age & Life Stage Insights

The majority of attrition is among employees aged 25–34.
📌 Younger employees may be exploring career options. Offering mentorship, training, and growth opportunities could help us retain this talent early.

Single employees are leaving more than others, suggesting mobility and flexibility are key factors.
📌 We can attract and retain them with career advancement programs, flexible policies, and engagement initiatives.



🌍 Diversity Snapshot

Gender distribution is balanced, meaning attrition is not heavily skewed by gender.
📌 This is great news—we can focus on other factors like department and role instead of gender-specific retention concerns.



🛠 Why This Dashboard Works

This dashboard isn’t just numbers—it’s a story of our people:

Easy-to-read KPIs (Total Employees, Attrition Rate, Average Age)

Visual charts and slicers for filtering by department and education

Reset button to instantly clear filters

Highlights who is most at risk so we can act fast



💡 Key Takeaways

1. Support Sales & Technical Roles: Focus on training, recognition, and career progression here first.

2. Engage Young Talent: Career paths, mentorship, and modern benefits can retain 25–34-year-olds.

3. Career Development: Bachelor’s/Master’s degree holders are a flight risk; invest in leadership pipelines.

4. Keep Listening: Regular surveys and exit interviews can uncover hidden reasons for attrition.

5. Celebrate Balance: Gender diversity is strong—keep nurturing it!
